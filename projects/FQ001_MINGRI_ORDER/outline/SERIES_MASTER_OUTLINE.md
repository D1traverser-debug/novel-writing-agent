## 0. Confidence classes

- `LOCKED`: identity/terminal constraint that may change only through explicit architecture re-open + impact analysis.

# I. Series identity and reader contract

## 1. One-line premise — LOCKED

夜间跑腿员陈渡每天零点会收到一张来自未来的订单；他可以改变订单指向的未来，却永远不知道自己改掉的是灾难，还是别人原本需要的结果。

## 2. Core reading contract — LOCKED

Every major unit should pay at least two of three appetites:

1. **本单真相** — 未来订单为什么要求这个物/人/时间/结果？
2. **改写代价** — 陈渡改变已知未来后，什么新的责任、损失、关系或风险被制造？
3. **来源终谜** — 谁/什么在选择可被陈渡看见的未来，为什么这些未来彼此冲突？

The book is not sustained by “another strange order.” It is sustained by progressively harder choices about **who gets to decide which future deserves to survive**.

## 3. Genre / experience — LOCKED

- 都市脑洞 × 悬疑单元剧 × 城市群像 × 长线价值观对抗。
- 超常只开一扇窄口；现实城市、职业、制度和人物必须持续成立。
- 爽点：信息差、临场选择、反转验证、能力/视野升级、用有限权限改变复杂系统。
- 不是：万能系统、纯刑侦、纯规则怪谈、无成本预知、纯哲学辩论。

## 4. Thematic problem — LOCKED

**知道未来，会不会自然获得替别人决定未来的权利？**

Competing values:
- 减少可预见伤害；
- 尊重当事人的知情与选择；
- 不作为也会制造后果；
- 信息越多，责任是否越大；
- 多数人的“更好结果”能否正当化少数人的被牺牲；
- 可预测是否等于可控制。

The series must force these values into costly choices, not speeches.

# II. Terminal constraints / ending design

## 5. Final dramatic question — LOCKED

When Chen Du finally gains the ability/opportunity to stabilize or privilege one future branch over competing alternatives, **will he accept the role of the person who decides everyone else’s tomorrow?**

## 6. Terminal choice — LOCKED FUNCTION, FLEX MECHANISM

Chen Du must reject both extremes:

- `CONTROL_ALL`: because he knows more, he monopolizes the future;
- `DO_NOTHING`: because uncertainty excuses all responsibility.

The terminal solution must embody:

> **承担选择造成的责任，但拒绝把“知道更多”变成永久替别人做决定的资格。**

Exact mechanism is `FLEX`. The ending cannot be solved merely by destroying one villain or switching Mingrida off.

## 7. Final cost — PLANNED

The terminal decision must cost Chen Du something he has spent the series learning to value: control certainty, a privileged future advantage, a relationship, social safety, or a guaranteed “best outcome.” No cost-free moral victory.

## 8. Ending-to-opening causal chain

Opening says: “Tomorrow has already written Chen Du’s death.”

Ending must answer: “Even if tomorrow can be written, **who has the right to write it for others?**”

Therefore every major volume must advance at least one of:
- Chen Du’s relation to control;
- who can author/select/send a future;
- whether “better future” can be objectively defined;
- whether consent survives asymmetric future knowledge.

# III. Global protagonist arc — Chen Du

## 9. Starting identity — LOCKED

- survives by reducing uncertainty;
- treats complex human problems as routing/dispatch problems;
- values controllability because his ordinary life gives him little structural power;
- emotionally restrained; responsibility feels safer when converted into procedure.

## 10. Limited belief — PLANNED

> “只要把变量查够、路线算清、风险压低，就能做出正确选择。”

This belief is useful early and therefore dangerous later. It makes him competent enough to survive but increasingly tempted to substitute calculation for consent.

## 11. Arc stages

### Stage A — Survive my future
He uses logistics intelligence to avoid his own predicted death.

Required change: future knowledge becomes actionable, but not trustworthy enough to equal truth.

### Stage B — My choice changes other people
Orders affect strangers, patients, drivers, victims, bystanders. He learns that “successful delivery” and “good outcome” are separate.

### Stage C — Let another person challenge my model
Xu Zhiwei becomes a durable falsification/consent pressure. Chen chooses whether to share uncertainty rather than merely data.

### Stage D — Another rational person chooses a different future
Gu Xingzhou/competing receiver demonstrates that Chen is not the only competent moral actor. Conflict shifts from information inequality to value conflict.

### Stage E — Winning creates unacceptable control
Chen becomes capable of predicting/manipulating larger chains. His original strength begins reproducing the danger he opposes.

### Stage F — Responsible uncertainty
Final transformation: he still acts under uncertainty, but no longer equates superior information with exclusive moral authority.

## 12. Arc proof points

Each volume must contain at least one irreversible choice in which Chen could have reduced his own uncertainty by controlling another person, but chooses/declines with visible cost. The choices must become progressively less private and less reversible.

# IV. Relationship architecture

## 13. Xu Zhiwei — evidence, consent, and human falsification

status: PLANNED DURABLE CO-LEAD PRESSURE ENGINE

Function:
- not “doctor who verifies facts for hero”;
- not automatic believer in Mingrida;
- embodies disciplined uncertainty: separate observation, inference, source, and decision;
- forces Chen to make predictions falsifiable before events occur;
- increasingly challenges whether being able to prevent an outcome gives him the right to manipulate a person into another.

Relationship arc:
1. suspicion / useful friction;
2. precommit verification partnership;
3. mutual dependence with asymmetric secrets;
4. moral disagreement when proof is strong but consent is weak;
5. trust rupture when Chen withholds/acts unilaterally for a “better” outcome;
6. rebuilt trust based on shared decision process, not blind faith in his information.

Romantic development is `FLEX`; relationship value must stand without romance.

## 14. Gu Xingzhou — competent counterargument

status: PLANNED PRIMARY IDEOLOGICAL OPPOSITION

Function:
- not a cackling villain;
- has evidence that some future branches cause wider harm;
- believes controlled withdrawal/stabilization of futures may be ethically necessary;
- may at times produce better measurable outcomes than Chen.

Escalation:
- unseen interference / second receiver signs;
- direct tactical rivalry;
- partial cooperation against a shared branch hazard;
- ideological split over acceptable sacrifice/consent;
- Gu’s strongest case should be emotionally and empirically credible before the final conflict.

## 15. Lao Ma — ordinary life / social cost anchor

status: LOCKED FUNCTION, FLEX PLOT

Function:
- proves Chen’s life has texture outside the mystery;
- anchors labor, money, fatigue, jokes, obligations and mutual care;
- makes “survive / disappear / become obsessed” socially consequential;
- should acquire his own decisions/problems, not become a comic exposition terminal.

Long-arc use:
- early: ordinary care and peer friction;
- middle: Chen’s secrecy/obsession damages ordinary trust/work;
- later: demonstrates costs that cannot be solved by future information alone.

## 16. Qin Lang — early consent/responsibility case

status: PLANNED EARLY ARC FUNCTION

His refusal of the key should become an early concrete lesson: an order can demand transfer to a person who explicitly refuses it. “Correct delivery” collides with agency before the series explains its grand ethics.

# V. World / Mingrida architecture

## 17. Worldbuilding principles — LOCKED

1. Real city first; supernatural aperture narrow.
2. New powers require consequence/limitation/exploit analysis before promotion.
3. Rule reveals move: OBSERVED → HYPOTHESIS → REPEATED → OPERATIONAL → CONFIRMED → BROKEN/REFRAMED.
4. Future information always creates information asymmetry, therefore social/ethical consequences.
5. Institutions retain realistic access, liability and workload constraints.
6. Expand consequences of existing rules before adding new mechanics.

## 18. Current operational rules — CANON-BOUND

Actual current rules live in Project Profile/Canon, including daily future order, sign/settlement distinction, deviation rate, future credit and limited readability. This outline does not override them.

## 19. Planned reveal ladder — PLANNED/HYPOTHESIS, NOT CANON

### R1 — Future can be changed
Front arc. Chen survives a predicted death; deviation is measurable but morally uninterpreted.

### R2 — Delivery condition != moral correctness
Orders can be physically satisfied while creating ambiguous consequences.

### R3 — Order source is not a single fixed tomorrow
Evidence accumulates that futures branch/recalculate rather than one immutable timeline simply “sending backward.”

### R4 — Multiple future interests can act
Second receiver / competing interventions prove there is counter-agency around which futures survive.

### R5 — “Future credit” changes access, not truth
More access means more possibilities/contradictions/responsibility—not a stronger oracle.

### R6 — Conflicting valid futures
Chen sees mutually incompatible orders with credible provenance. System cannot be treated as one authoritative narrator.

### R7 — Sender architecture
`HYPOTHESIS`: orders may emerge from agents/processes inside future branches rather than a single central intelligence. Exact ontology remains open until evidence architecture is designed.

### R8 — Selection/stabilization problem
Late series: the conflict becomes whether a branch can/should be privileged, not merely how to interpret one order.

### R9 — Terminal reframing
Final reveal must clarify why the opening self-sent death order was possible while deepening—not trivializing—the consent/control theme.

## 20. World consequence checklist at each new rule

For every promoted Mingrida/world capability, answer before prose:
- what can Chen now do that he could not do;
- what new mistake becomes possible;
- who else benefits or is harmed;
- what institution/social behavior would realistically change if exposed;
- what exploit or adversarial tactic appears;
- what previous assumption becomes false;
- what limitation keeps conflict alive.

# VI. Mystery architecture

## 21. Mystery tiers

### Tier 1 — Unit truth
Why this order, person, object, deadline, or refusal?
Pay frequently.

### Tier 2 — Rule truth
What does signing, deviation, credit, branch conflict actually mean?
Pay periodically; each answer must create a harder operational problem.

### Tier 3 — Actor truth
Who else knows/acts around Mingrida? What does Gu Xingzhou know? Who sends/intercepts orders?
Pay at volume scale.

### Tier 4 — Ontological truth
What constitutes a “future branch,” who can select it, and why can Chen receive from it?
Pay late-series.

### Tier 5 — Moral truth
Even when mechanics are understood, who should choose? This is answered by climax choices, not exposition.

## 22. Reveal discipline

- Design major reveals solution-first, then plant fair clues/alternate hypotheses backward.
- A reveal must reinterpret at least one prior clue/event.
- No answer may exist only because the author withheld a fact that the POV character obviously knew.
- Long mystery must not use “more unexplained nouns” as progress.

# VII. Series / volume roadmap

Target total length around 1.2M remains a commercial planning baseline, **not a fixed six-volume word quota**. Volume boundaries can move if story evidence requires it.

## Volume 1 — 《明天已经发生》 — PLANNED

Primary transformation:
Chen proves the future can be changed, learns successful interference is not the same as good outcome, builds Xu as a skeptical verification partner, and encounters evidence of a second actor/receiver.

World progress:
R1 → R2 → early R3/R4.

Character progress:
self-survival → responsibility for others → first willingness to be challenged.

Major end state:
Mingrida exposes two mutually exclusive “valid” futures / 48h-scale conflict (exact UI/mechanism FLEX). Chen can no longer think the system points toward a single correct tomorrow.

## Volume 2 — 《两种正确的明天》 — PLANNED

Primary engine:
competing branches + Gu Xingzhou tactical/ethical rivalry.

Questions:
- when both futures prevent a different harm, which intervention is “failure”?
- can two people with partial future information cooperate?

World progress:
R4 → R5 → R6.

Relationship pressure:
Xu demands stronger precommit/consent standards as Chen’s information power grows.

End state:
Chen wins an important branch conflict but discovers his “better” outcome imposed a serious invisible cost on someone who never consented.

## Volume 3 — 《谁在寄出未来》 — PLANNED

Primary engine:
sender/source investigation widens beyond one city-night logistics loop while remaining grounded in human/institutional consequences.

World progress:
R7 hypotheses tested; multiple actors/networks/institutions may encounter branch artifacts.

Risk:
do not become pure lore investigation. Every source answer must affect a living relationship/choice.

End state:
Chen learns enough about sender architecture to intentionally influence what information reaches him—but doing so also changes which futures remain observable.

## Volume 4 — 《知道以后》 — PLANNED

Primary engine:
future knowledge spreads or becomes socially consequential; consent/privacy/authority move from individual cases to networks/institutions.

Character pressure:
Chen’s habit of control becomes visibly harmful; Xu relationship reaches major ideological rupture.

World progress:
R7 → R8.

End state:
A seemingly responsible large-scale intervention prevents one disaster but locks in another serious irreversible loss.

## Volume 5 — 《唯一正确未来》 — PLANNED

Primary engine:
branch-selection ideology becomes explicit. Gu/other actors argue that uncontrolled branching is itself catastrophic.

Character pressure:
Chen is now powerful/experienced enough to become what he once feared: a person who can rationally justify choosing for others.

Required event function:
he obtains a credible opportunity to stabilize/control futures and is initially tempted to use it.

End state:
irreversible public-scale loss or relationship fracture proves neither “control everything” nor “never intervene” survives reality.

## Volume 6 — 《明天不属于任何人》 — PLANNED TERMINAL VOLUME

Primary engine:
resolve sender paradox / branch-selection mechanism / opening death-order logic while forcing terminal moral choice.

Required closure:
- opening self-order recontextualized;
- Gu’s strongest argument answered through action, not defeat-by-speech;
- Xu relationship reaches its final consent/trust form;
- Lao Ma/ordinary-life thread demonstrates what future agency is for;
- Chen acts decisively without claiming permanent monopoly over other people’s futures.

Final image/event is FLEX; final moral transformation is LOCKED.

# IX. Escalation and anti-repetition architecture

## 23. Escalation dimensions

Rotate rather than stack all at once:
- information depth;
- personal/relationship cost;
- moral ambiguity;
- time pressure;
- public scale;
- rule complexity;
- opponent sophistication;
- irreversibility.

Adjacent units should not use the same primary engine + same dominant cost + same reveal shape.

## 24. Order-function rotation

Possible functions include delivery, retrieval, delay, no-contact placement, escort, custody, verification, intentional failure, choosing among incompatible deliveries. This is a design palette, not a quota.

## 25. Human engine requirement

Every unit must contain at least one durable or temporary person whose independent goal can change the plot without serving as an information terminal.

## 26. Breather requirement

After sustained high pressure, a lower-tension sequence still needs at least two of:
- relationship movement;
- consequence/aftershock;
- ordinary-life attachment;
- future conflict loading;
- changed decision/resource.

## 31. Re-open rule

Re-open this master outline when:
- real reader/platform evidence contradicts current architecture;
- a Canon contradiction makes the plan impossible;
