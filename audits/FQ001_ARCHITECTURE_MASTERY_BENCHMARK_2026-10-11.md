# 2. C01 — Premise / reader-contract benchmark

## Contract

Core promise is not “每天一个奇怪订单.” It is:

> Future information gives Chen Du a temporary advantage but never a complete moral answer; each order forces a choice about changing a future whose full causality he cannot know.

Reader appetites:
- unit truth;
- consequence of intervention;
- source/branch mystery.

## Alternative challenge

A. “Pure death-countdown thriller” — stronger immediate hook, too narrow for 1.2M and makes later orders feel like dilution.

B. “Infinite strange-order anthology” — easy unit generation, weak cumulative character/world architecture.

C. “Urban future-order mystery + escalating agency/consent problem” — selected because it supports both immediate unit payoff and long-form transformation.

Falsifier: if future arcs stop making choices harder and only add stranger objects/rules, C01 fails even if clicks remain acceptable.

Result: L3 for project architecture.

# 3. C02 — Series architecture benchmark

## Ending-to-opening chain

Opening: “Tomorrow already contains Chen’s death.”

Front arc answer: future can be altered.

Vol1 answer: altered future is not automatically better; another actor may prefer another branch.

Middle-series pressure: increased access makes Chen more capable of choosing for others.

Late-series pressure: a credible case emerges for branch stabilization/control.

Terminal answer: knowledge creates responsibility but does not automatically create permanent authority over others’ futures.

## Competing macro architectures

### A. Order-of-the-week for hundreds of chapters
Rejected: strong repeatability, weak escalating argument and high engine fatigue.

### B. Early full conspiracy/lore reveal
Rejected: burns mystery runway, converts grounded city suspense into exposition/institutional thriller too early.

### C. Nested scale growth
Unit → relationship verification → counter-agent → competing branches → sender architecture → social/ethical branch-selection conflict.
Selected.

## Flex test

Exact volume length, objects, temporary cases and sender ontology are FLEX/HYPOTHESIS. Ending pressure, Chen’s transformation, Xu/Gu functions and narrow-aperture identity are stronger constraints.

This prevents outline rigidity while preserving causality.

Result: L3.

# 4. C03 — World / Mingrida implication benchmark

## Capability: future order gives useful but incomplete information

### Direct advantage
Chen can act before a future event/condition would otherwise be knowable.

### Limitation
- order does not explain causality or moral quality;
- other people cannot simply inspect the anomaly as shared objective UI evidence;
- changing reality changes downstream conditions;
- current access remains constrained by ordinary institutions.

### New mistake enabled
Chen can overfit to a predicted outcome, manipulate people based on inaccessible evidence, or preserve one danger while moving another.

### Exploit surface
- hostile/cooperative actors can manipulate current-world conditions around delivery;
- if another actor knows order mechanics, they can target object/time/recipient dependencies rather than fight Chen physically;
- Chen can be induced to treat an order as recommendation when it may only describe/select an outcome.

### Social/institutional consequences if exposed
- medical/legal/police/insurance/employment decisions would demand provenance and reproducibility that Chen cannot supply;
- prediction could create liability and consent problems;
- powerful actors would seek privileged access, making secrecy itself a moral choice.

### Escalation without adding arbitrary power
Use existing mechanics more deeply: conflicting branches, competing actors, shifted consequences, precommit evidence, wider stakes. Do not solve with a new “truth indicator.”

## Capability: deviation/future credit

Rule design constraint:
- deviation may measure difference, not goodness;
- credit may widen access, not certainty;
- any expansion must create new ambiguity/cost/exploit, not simply upgrade Chen into an oracle.

## Late sender ontology
Still HYPOTHESIS. The master outline explicitly refuses to Canonize whether Mingrida is a central intelligence, future-human network, branch protocol or another mechanism before fair evidence architecture exists.

Result: L3 for current design boundaries; no false claim that late ontology is solved.

# 5. C04 — Chen Du decisive-choice map

1. **Survival choice:** intervene in route/risk despite incomplete proof — proves competence and control habit are useful.
2. **Key refusal/consent choice:** order says deliver; recipient says no — first collision between system condition and another person’s agency.
3. **Verification relationship choice:** allow Xu to precommit/falsify his claims — gives up unilateral epistemic control.
4. **Bad-order choice:** consider intentional failure because delivery appears harmful — separates obedience from responsibility.
5. **Competing-future choice:** choose under two credible incompatible outcomes — destroys fantasy of one calculable right route.
6. **Mid-series control temptation:** privileged information makes unilateral intervention measurably effective — flaw becomes attractive again.
7. **Terminal choice:** act responsibly without monopolizing future selection.

Failure test: if a major turn can be replaced with “Chen obtains one more fact and therefore knows the answer,” character arc is not driving plot.

Result: L3.

# 7. C06/C07 — Theme, opposition and stakes benchmark

Competing positions must all be capable of causing both good and harm:

- Chen early: reduce uncertainty and intervene case-by-case.
- Xu: evidence/consent/professional boundary before causal certainty.
- Gu: some futures may need active suppression/stabilization to prevent larger harms.
- ordinary affected people: may rationally prefer autonomy even when Chen predicts a worse outcome.

Escalation is not “bigger explosion.” It rotates:
individual survival → responsibility for stranger → durable trust → competing actor → distributed harm → institutional/social consequence → branch-level authority.

Gu becomes a valid counterargument only if at least one major measurable outcome supports his method better than Chen’s before the late-series ideological split.

Result: L3.

# 8. C08 — Mystery / information benchmark

## Front-arc truth ladder

Question A: Is the death order real enough to act on?
- clue: impossible/self-addressed order + current-world corroboration;
- competing explanation: prank/system corruption;
- payoff: predicted danger window produces a real causal collision.

Question B: Can the future be changed?
- clue: intervention changes route/conditions;
- payoff: Chen survives 23:46 and deviation is nonzero.

Question C: Does “successful intervention” mean good outcome?
- seed: anonymous timing interference / moved risks;
- second-order evidence: key order targets a person who refuses; patient timing defeats simplest rescue theory;
- front-packet reframe: the order’s target condition and moral value separate.

Question D: Who can know/manipulate the order chain?
- seed early human interference without explaining identity;
- later counter-agent/receiver line pays person-level agency.

## Long mystery boundary

The final ontology of sender/self-order after predicted death is not solved yet. Therefore series-level C08 remains L2 for that specific ontology until a solution-first fair-play ladder is built. This does **not** block the current CH1–8 rebase because the front-arc mystery only needs fair current evidence and must avoid falsely confirming the late answer.

Result: front arc L3; late ontology L2.

# 14. Adversarial architecture challenge

## Attack 1 — The new series outline may over-intellectualize the book
Risk: consent/future-authority theme can turn scenes into philosophy.

Control: every thematic beat must be embodied in an object/person/deadline/choice; no volume may be carried by debates without practical consequence.

## Attack 2 — Xu arrives earlier but may still function only as a verification tool
Control: give Xu independent medical/professional priorities and later disagreement where her choice materially blocks/changes Chen’s plan.

## Attack 3 — Gu may become a deferred “cool villain” promise with no concrete pressure
Control: seed counter-agency before full reveal; by direct arrival he must already have changed an outcome, not merely explain lore.

## Attack 4 — World mystery may be protected by vagueness rather than fair planning
Control: before any late ontology reveal is drafted, build a solution-first clue ledger and mark what the reader could retrospectively infer. Current HYPOTHESIS status is safer than pretending the answer exists.

## Attack 5 — Moving first payoff to CH3 may feel mechanically rushed
Control: CH3 placement is a current target, not a sacred number. The gate judges promise/progress/payoff and emotional credibility, not chapter index. Architecture may shift if prototype evidence shows CH4 or another boundary reads better.

## Attack 6 — Six-volume roadmap may become a cage
Control: volume functions/transformations bind more strongly than exact cases/counts. Re-open on real reader/platform/Canon evidence.

Adversarial result: no current BLOCKER; key HIGH risks are converted into explicit production gates rather than hidden assumptions.
