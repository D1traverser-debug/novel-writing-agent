## 6. Why the previous repair matrix is no longer the active optimum

The previous matrix prioritized `CH4 > CH2 > CH3 > CH1` and deliberately minimized continuity/migration cost. That was rational while already-published text was treated as expensive to replace.

The user has now changed the impact boundary: the entire front arc may be withdrawn/reworked. Under the new objective function, the migration-cost advantage of local patching collapses.

Second-order challenge of the old accepted plan:
- it can make each chapter cleaner while preserving the same delayed CH5 payoff;
- it can preserve the same late CH8/CH9 relationship engine;
- local compression can leave the acquisition packet's macro cadence unchanged;
- optimizing chapter-by-chapter risks a locally improved but globally similar first 20k.

Disposition: **supersede targeted-patch plan for active execution; retain it as historical learning evidence.**
