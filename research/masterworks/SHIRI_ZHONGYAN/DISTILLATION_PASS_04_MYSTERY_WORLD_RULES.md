## 0. Question

How does the work make a very large rule/mystery system feel expandable rather than arbitrary, and which parts of that design should *not* be copied into FQ001?

Target competencies: C02 series architecture, C03 world/system design, C07 opposition/stakes, C08 mystery/information architecture, C15 foreshadow/payoff, C16 domain research, C20 meta-learning.

## 1. Mystery design starts from a “bottom,” not from endless withholding

The author publicly describes a planning process in which a substantial part of the overall outline existed before drafting and planted questions generally had intended fill/payoff directions. For individual games, he also describes simulating obvious solutions, treating the first solution as something the rule-maker should already anticipate, then redesigning from the opponent/rule-maker perspective.

Distilled design loop:
`intended truth/bottom -> obvious player model -> rule-maker counter -> participant counter-counter -> fair evidence -> reveal -> new consequence`.

The transferable lesson is not “make everything hard to guess.” It is **the author must know more causal structure than the reader sees**.

FQ001 benchmark:
For every major Mingrida reveal, maintain a private answer/ontology candidate and a clue/payoff map before planting high-cost clues. No major long-horizon clue may be introduced solely because it sounds mysterious.

## 2. Rule design is adversarial, not declarative

A rule becomes story when characters can:
- misunderstand it;
- exploit it;
- test it;
- pay for breaking it;
- discover that the rule-maker anticipated a strategy;
- create a new tactic under the same constraints.

This makes the world feel like an active system rather than a wiki.

Rule benchmark:
For each important rule, generate:
1. naive reading;
2. obvious exploit;
3. countermeasure/opposition response;
4. edge case;
5. human/moral consequence;
6. what evidence would force a reframe.

If a rule only supports exposition, it is not carrying enough story weight.

## 3. “Expand before add” appears in practice

The work repeatedly derives new consequences from a finite base of recurring concepts (cycle, memory, games, hierarchy, abilities, identity, rules) instead of solving every new problem by inventing an unrelated magic subsystem.

Transfer principle:
Before adding a new Mingrida mechanic, ask whether the desired effect can be produced by:
- a new consequence of an existing rule;
- conflict between two existing rules;
- different stakeholder interpretation;
- information asymmetry;
- an exploit or failure mode.

FQ001 should prefer deeper consequences per rule over more terminology.

## 4. Worldbuilding has social roles, not only mechanics

Public criticism/analysis of the work points out that participant/hierarchy/rule-maker identities form social positions, not merely game labels. Advancement/transformation changes relation to self, others and the system.

Worldbuilding audit:
A speculative institution/mechanic should affect at least two of:
- incentives;
- status/power;
- trust;
- labor/resource flow;
- identity;
- consent;
- liability;
- memory/history;
- who can know what;
- who gets punished/rewarded.

This is how high-concept rules acquire thematic and social depth.

## 5. Terminology can be viewpoint-bound

A term can be useful and “true enough” at one stage while later being recontextualized as one actor/group’s label for a larger phenomenon.

Important fair-play constraint:
Later recontextualization must deepen earlier evidence rather than nullify it by author fiat.

Good reframe:
- earlier observations remain factually valid;
- causal meaning changes;
- some prior oddities become newly legible.

Bad reframe:
- new ontology contradicts old scenes with no planted exception;
- explanation relies on information no participant/reader could ever infer;
- every contradiction is dismissed as “the system can do anything.”

## 6. Information asymmetry can replace numeric leveling

One distinctive long-serial engine is that characters differ not only by power, but by memory/information horizon. That means “progress” can be:
- remembering more;
- realizing another person knows more;
- discovering a memory is unreliable;
- joining information across relationships;
- learning the cost of knowing.

FQ001 transfer:
Mingrida’s future information should not become a simple level-up ladder where more credit = objectively more truth. Better progression candidates include:
- wider future visibility but more conflicting branches;
- more information but higher ethical responsibility;
- knowing a risk without knowing consent/preferences;
- different actors seeing incompatible but locally valid futures.

## 7. Every local game/unit should feed the mainline

High-signal critical discussion of the work notes that side games often contain mainline anchors rather than existing as disposable dungeons.

Packet audit:
Each unit should advance at least one long debt:
- world rule;
- origin mystery;
- opposition model;
- character transformation;
- relationship state;
- ethical argument;
- major promise/payoff.

If a unit resets all of these, it is filler even if the local puzzle is clever.

FQ001 implication:
A future order cannot be judged only by whether “this order is interesting.” It must also advance the long engine.

## 8. Mystery ladder: answer -> larger consequence, not answer -> more nouns

A sustainable nested mystery should obey:
`question A -> evidence -> partial answer A -> consequence/reframe -> question B that matters more because A was answered`.

Weak version:
`question A -> no answer -> question B -> question C -> terminology D`.

Debt audit fields:
- owner question;
- current hypotheses;
- evidence planted;
- next allowed reveal window;
- promised payoff horizon;
- what changes when answered;
- debt risk if postponed.

This directly guards against “mystery debris.”

## 9. Theme should emerge from rules under pressure

The work’s rules are often interesting because they force questions of fairness, sacrifice, memory, identity, rule-following and resistance. The general lesson is that a good speculative mechanic can be a **moral pressure machine**.

FQ001 rule consequence triad:
1. mechanical: what can/cannot happen;
2. behavioral/social: what people now do because of it;
3. moral/relationship: what hard choice becomes possible/necessary.

A Mingrida rule with only #1 should not automatically survive architecture review.

## 10. Complexity is not the goal

The author explicitly describes complexity as a personal differentiator. That is valuable evidence of *author strategy*, not a universal craft law.

Counterevidence from reader criticism shows the same complex-information style can be experienced as dragging, over-centralized cleverness, excessive mystery debt or late explanatory burden.

Anti-overfit rule:
**complexity must purchase reader value** — a harder choice, stronger reframe, deeper relationship, richer consequence or fairer payoff. If it only increases the amount to remember, simplify.

## 11. FQ001 transfer experiments

### E7 — Mingrida rule implication tree
For each active rule, map 1st-order, 2nd-order and social/ethical consequences. Prefer one existing-rule consequence over a new rule when equivalent.

### E8 — Answer-aware clue ledger
For every CH1–CH8 long clue, specify intended current answer candidate, alternative hypothesis, fair observable clue and reframe path. Remove “cool mystery” clues with no payoff direction.

### E9 — Information-asymmetry escalation
Design at least two future-order conflicts where more information makes Chen Du’s choice harder rather than simply stronger.

### E10 — Unit-to-mainline anchor
Every order/unit must name its longline contribution before drafting. If none exists, merge/delete/reconceptualize.

### E11 — Rule-maker adversarial simulation
For one Mingrida rule, simulate Chen’s obvious exploit and an intelligent counter; ensure the conflict remains within existing world capabilities.

## 12. Promotion decisions

### REFERENCE
- answer-aware long mystery planning;
- rule-maker / participant adversarial simulation;
- viewpoint-bound terminology with fair recontextualization;
- information horizon as progression dimension;
- local-unit-to-mainline anchor;
- world mechanics as social/moral structure.

### EXPERIMENTAL FOR FQ001
- Mingrida implication tree;
- answer-aware clue ledger;
- information-asymmetry escalation;
- unit-to-mainline anchor gate;
- rule-maker adversarial simulation.

### NOT PROMOTED
- complexity as an intrinsic virtue;
- fifty-plus powers/abilities as a target;
- copying生肖/天地人/轮回/回响 structures;
- hiding the answer simply to avoid prediction;
- protagonist omniscience as compensation for world complexity.
