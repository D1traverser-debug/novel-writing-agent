## 0. Goal

This pass asks a narrower question than Pass 01:

> In the opening packet, what concrete prose/scene mechanisms create attention, pressure, character distinction and forward motion — and which of those mechanisms can survive transfer into FQ001 without copying the source work?

Target competencies: C09 scene, C10 chapter/hooks, C12 dialogue/subtext, C13 POV/immersion, C14 emotion, C16 domain/reality selection, plus C08 information architecture.

## 1. Cold-read experience map

The opening does not ask the reader to memorize the whole world. It repeatedly narrows attention to one urgent model problem, then destabilizes that model.

Observed reader-state sequence at a functional level:
1. Where am I / why is this room wrong?
2. Who controls this place and what happens if the rules are ignored?
3. Which participant is lying?
4. Are the individual stories actually independent?
5. What is the protagonist hiding?
6. Is the apparent local game even the real problem?
7. If the first explanation is true, what larger world must exist outside this room?

Lesson: acquisition is produced by **question turnover**, not by endlessly stacking unanswered nouns.

FQ001 benchmark:
- each replacement chapter records its dominant reader question at open and close;
- a chapter passes only if it answers, reframes or materially escalates at least one live question;
- adding a new Mingrida term without changing the reader model does not count as progression.

## 2. Psychological portrayal

### 2.1 Attention is character
The protagonist is characterized first by *what he notices* and how quickly he turns observation into a provisional model.

General mechanism:
`stimulus -> selective attention -> interpretation -> contradiction -> revised model -> decision`

This does two jobs at once:
- moves the mystery;
- shows the mind of the viewpoint character.

### 2.2 Emotion leaks through control failure
Fear/shock is often more convincing when the otherwise controlled cognitive process is interrupted by involuntary bodily or behavioral leakage.

General mechanism:
`control strategy -> pressure exceeds strategy -> body/voice/action leaks -> character recovers or changes plan`

This is more useful than long labels such as “he was terrified” when the viewpoint character is habitually restrained.

### 2.3 Private stakes prevent puzzle-machine syndrome
The protagonist is not only solving an abstract room. A private survival/return motive gives the reasoning emotional direction.

FQ001 transfer:
Chen Du’s psychology must not become “logistics Sherlock narration.” His useful pattern is:
`notice operational anomaly -> map risk -> suppress personal implication -> a human consequence breaks the model -> choose with incomplete control -> show aftershock`.

Failure test:
If a paragraph of internal thought can be transferred unchanged from Chen Du to any generic intelligent protagonist, it fails voice/psychology specificity.

## 3. Environment and spatial description

### 3.1 Multi-function density
Effective opening environment details simultaneously do several jobs:
- locate bodies and exits;
- make the space feel wrong;
- produce evidence;
- constrain action;
- foreshadow danger;
- reveal what the viewpoint character prioritizes.

This creates a high information-per-line ratio without reading like a report because the details are selected under pressure.

### 3.2 Focalized atmosphere
Atmosphere is not a separate ornamental layer. The same physical element can feel different because character pressure selects and interprets it differently.

FQ001 environment tags for candidate prose:
- `ORIENTATION`
- `CONSTRAINT`
- `CLUE`
- `THREAT`
- `CHARACTER_LENS`
- `EMOTION`
- `WORLD`
- `FORESHADOW`
- `ACTION_SURFACE`

A paragraph with no tag is not automatically deleted, but its page-cost must be justified.

### 3.3 Spatial precision must earn itself
The opening uses spatial facts because they matter to confinement/inference. This is the opposite of FQ001’s previous failure mode where route/time precision sometimes accumulated without changing the model.

Transfer rule candidate:
**Precision is valuable when it changes orientation, inference, choice or consequence. Precision without state change is cognitive tax.**

## 4. Dialogue and voice

### 4.1 Dialogue is adversarial information exchange
People are not taking turns providing clean answers. They protect identity, challenge credibility, misread each other, reveal pressure and negotiate trust.

Useful variables per dialogue scene:
- speaker objective;
- what they refuse to say;
- what they want the other person to believe;
- current power/trust position;
- what changes after the exchange.

### 4.2 Exposition hidden inside stakes
Backstory is useful because the present game makes the truth-status of the backstory consequential.

Transfer rule candidate:
When FQ001 needs exposition, bind it to a present decision:
- Will Chen trust this person?
- Will Xu Zhiwei accept the evidence?
- Does this memory alter consent, danger, leverage or the interpretation of an order?

If the answer is “no, the reader merely learns something,” compress or relocate.

### 4.3 Voice is not catchphrases
Distinctiveness comes from what characters defend, dodge, notice, infer and risk—not only from vocabulary tics.

FQ001 character-voice benchmark:
Remove speaker labels from a tense 8–12 line exchange. A reviewer should identify speakers primarily from goals, values and response patterns, not gimmicky slang.

## 5. Plot progression

### 5.1 State change beats event count
The opening proves that a story can advance in a constrained location if important state variables change.

Track per scene:
- GOAL
- KNOWLEDGE
- RELATIONSHIP
- RISK
- RESOURCE
- LOCATION
- WORLD_MODEL
- MORAL_COMMITMENT

A scene in which none changes is a deletion/merge candidate.

### 5.2 Verification must threaten the model
Repeated checking becomes dramatic only when a check can invalidate a working theory or force a different action.

This directly corrects FQ001’s old front-arc issue:
`query -> confirmation -> query -> confirmation` is not suspense.

Better structure:
`working model -> test -> unexpected result -> cost -> revised model -> new choice`.

### 5.3 Partial answers should create consequential uncertainty
The opening frequently resolves a local question while exposing a larger one.

This differs from mystery debt inflation. A valid reframe must:
- reduce one uncertainty;
- create a more consequential uncertainty;
- remain causally connected to the answer just obtained.

## 6. Chapter architecture / hooks

Chapter-end hook taxonomy distilled from function rather than copied events:
- `DANGER_CHANGE`
- `MODEL_REVERSAL`
- `IDENTITY_REVEAL`
- `RELATIONSHIP_RUPTURE`
- `CHOICE_PENDING`
- `ANSWER_OPENS_LARGER_QUESTION`
- `COST_LANDS`
- `WORLD_EXPANDS`

FQ001 guardrail:
No more than two consecutive chapters in the replacement packet should depend on the same dominant hook type unless a reviewer explicitly justifies escalation rather than repetition.

First-screen benchmark:
The opening screen should establish at least two of:
- abnormality;
- active desire/problem;
- character-specific perception;
- imminent consequence;
- unresolved contradiction.

This is a benchmark, not a mandatory sentence-count formula.

## 7. Emotion and attachment

The opening’s tension would become sterile if every character were only a puzzle function. Personal reasons, conflicting memories, bodily vulnerability and later recontextualization turn information into attachment.

FQ001 transfer:
Every high-concept order sequence should contain a human attachment vector:
- who loses something;
- who refuses the “optimal” answer;
- who remembers the consequence;
- whose relationship changes after Chen intervenes.

Aftershock requirement:
When a chapter contains serious harm, betrayal, near death or moral compromise, the packet must budget an aftershock beat somewhere appropriate. Immediate reset to the next puzzle is a quality risk.

## 8. Exposition / worldbuilding

A rule should preferably be learned through at least one of:
- consequence;
- failed attempt;
- disagreement;
- exploit;
- surprise;
- costly choice.

Pure explanation is acceptable when clarity demands it, but it should not be the default discovery engine.

For Mingrida, every meaningful rule candidate gets a consequence triad:
1. mechanical consequence;
2. behavioral/social consequence;
3. moral/relationship consequence.

If only #1 exists, the rule is under-exploited or unnecessary.

## 9. Paragraph / mobile-reading rhythm

Do not imitate the source’s sentence surface. Distill the functional rhythm instead:
- short paragraphs accelerate threat/decision;
- slightly longer blocks can hold inference or emotional landing;
- paragraph breaks often correspond to changed attention, speaker, inference or beat;
- repeated one-line emphasis loses force when overused.

FQ001 benchmark:
Review a mobile-width render and mark whether paragraphing reflects beat changes rather than arbitrary “web-novel chopping.”

## 10. Counterfactual / ablation tests

### Test A — Remove the functional environment
If the opening room became a generic dark room with no spatial anomaly/clue function, atmosphere might remain but the mystery engine weakens sharply.
Conclusion: environment-clue coupling is causal, not decorative.

### Test B — Replace hypothesis progression with explanation
If a narrator simply explains the room/game/world rules early, comprehension rises but reader participation and distrust collapse.
Conclusion: staged inference has value when the reader has enough fair evidence to participate.

### Test C — Make every participant answer cleanly
Backstory remains, but conflict, credibility pressure and character voice flatten.
Conclusion: information should often arrive through goal conflict rather than interview compliance.

### Test D — End every chapter on physical danger
Short-term urgency may rise, but hook variety and mystery/relationship texture collapse.
Conclusion: “next-click reason” is the invariant; cliffhanger danger is only one implementation.
