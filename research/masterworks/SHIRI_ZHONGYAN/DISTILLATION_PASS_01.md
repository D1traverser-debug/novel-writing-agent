## 2. Author-process findings

### 2.1 Outline before mystery
Author says roughly 60% of the outline existed before drafting, was repeatedly overturned/rehearsed/developed, and was completed during serialization. He states that when planting a pit/clue he already had a fill/payoff direction.

Distillation hypothesis:
- mystery-first serial fiction benefits from answer-aware planning;
- outline should be complete enough to constrain clues but flexible enough to absorb better local solutions;
- `pit planted -> payoff idea known -> later detail refined` is safer than `pit planted -> hope to invent answer later`.

Promotion: REFERENCE -> project experiment candidate for C02/C08/C15.

### 2.2 Learn the real domain needed by the character
Author reports deliberately studying combat after earlier criticism and researching concepts/knowledge because the protagonist needed to appear genuinely knowledgeable.

Distillation hypothesis:
- research should be triggered by a dramatic requirement, not by “more detail is always better”;
- domain knowledge must change tactics/choices, not become decorative terminology.

Promotion: REFERENCE for C16.

### 2.3 Reader feedback as a sensor, not an oracle
Author closely watched reader comments, used dense paragraph-comment activity as a signal of strong moments, and adjusted some character screen time based on response.

Distillation hypothesis:
- reader feedback can expose blind spots and attachment peaks;
- comments should be clustered by recurring mechanism, not followed one-by-one;
- feedback can reopen hypotheses but cannot silently rewrite Canon or replace architecture reasoning.

Promotion: REFERENCE for C18/C20.

### 2.4 Character familiarity improves prose
In a later creation essay, author says his later prose/emotional progression/character shaping became stronger as he became more familiar with the characters; environment and atmosphere also increasingly emerged from the characters.

Distillation hypothesis:
- character model quality affects not only dialogue but perception, environment, pacing and emotional texture;
- early-volume character simulation deserves dedicated benchmark before prose freeze.

Promotion: EXPERIMENTAL for C04/C05/C12/C13/C14.

## 3. Opening packet — chapters 1–10

### 3.1 First chapter: environment is functional
The opening room does multiple jobs immediately:
- orientation: closed, old, abnormal room;
- atmosphere: stale/dim/rotting sensory field;
- clue: no door, gridded surfaces, clock, participant count;
- threat: masked host and physical helplessness;
- cognitive engine: protagonist notices contradictions;
- chapter payoff: the participant-count discrepancy becomes lethal proof that rules matter.

The environment is therefore not a detached paragraph of visual decoration. It changes what the protagonist can infer and what the reader fears.

FQ001 experiment:
- every front-arc environment paragraph must be tagged with at least one functional role;
- generic city realism that changes no decision/inference/emotion is a page-cost suspect.

### 3.2 Psychological portrayal: cognition under pressure
Early Qi Xia psychology is mostly built through:
- selective attention to anomalies;
- hypothesis formation;
- probability/strategy shifts;
- involuntary physical reaction when violence exceeds his tolerance;
- private objective (“must survive / someone waits outside”);
- willingness to change plan when rule interpretation changes.

It is not primarily built by long abstract self-description.

FQ001 experiment:
For Chen Du, replace “he felt anxious / confused / restrained” with a controlled chain where possible:
`what he notices -> what model it activates -> what he refuses to admit -> what he chooses -> what cost leaks into body/behavior`.

Do not copy Qi Xia’s hyper-analytical voice; Chen Du’s cognition should remain logistics-shaped and fallible.

### 3.3 Exposition doubles as conflict evidence
Ch.3–7 embed participant backstories inside a lethal truth/lie game. Character exposition therefore has two simultaneous jobs:
- reveal identity/voice/past;
- provide evidence for the current puzzle.

This avoids a common “pause plot to explain character” split.

Transfer principle:
- whenever backstory appears in FQ001, ask what present-tense decision, trust, suspicion, leverage or clue it changes now.

### 3.4 Chapter endings change the active question
Observed opening sequence pattern:
- ch1: participant-count anomaly becomes violent rule proof;
- ch2: protagonist identity card changes survival objective;
- ch3: apparent easy strategy creates false confidence;
- ch4–7: separate stories become a connected-event problem;
- ch8: protagonist reveals his hidden role, changing group dynamics;
- ch9: local liar-game question expands into a larger ontology question about what happened to all participants;
- ch10: first answer does not close the world; it opens the next escape/world question.

Core mechanism is not “always end on danger.” It is:
`current question -> partial answer/reframe -> new higher-value next question`.

FQ001 experiment:
- chapter-end audit tracks `OPENED / ANSWERED / REFRAMED / ESCALATED` questions;
- repeated “new order arrives” hooks fail engine-diversity review unless state meaning changes.

### 3.5 Progression is hypothesis progression
In the opening, the room barely changes physically for many chapters, yet plot still advances because the reader model changes repeatedly.

This demonstrates a useful distinction:
- event density != progression;
- progression can be a meaningful change in knowledge, trust, survival plan, group power, or world model.

FQ001 implication:
- procedural verification scenes are not automatically slow; they become slow when each verification confirms the same model instead of changing it.

## 4. Character / ensemble findings

### 4.1 Strong characters are not just trait bundles
Selected later character chapter openings use a recurring “I lied” recontextualization of first-room testimony. The device converts earlier functional information into a personal moral history.

Mechanism:
`known surface identity -> confession of distortion -> private history -> revised interpretation of current behavior`.

Strength:
- creates retrospective depth;
- rewards reader memory;
- allows side characters to become protagonists temporarily.

Risk:
- repeated autobiography format can become engine repetition if every character earns depth the same way.

### 4.2 Character attachment can outrank metaphysical logic
Author’s later interview explicitly notes readers cared intensely about a particular lived version/state of characters even when the metaphysical model argued continuity.

Transfer principle:
- emotional continuity and ontological continuity are different;
- if a rule resets/replaces/mutates a person, the story must track the reader’s attachment object, not only explain metaphysics.

FQ001 relevance:
- future-order changes must affect recognizable human relationships/lives, not only abstract “timeline deviation.”

### 4.3 “Character comes alive” is a benchmark problem
Author describes later characters producing surprising-but-consistent dialogue/actions once he knew them well enough.

NOVEL_OS benchmark proposal:
Before a major character drives a high-impact scene, run three candidate choices under the same pressure:
- one plot-convenient;
- one trait-stereotyped;
- one drive/relationship-consistent.
Reviewer must identify why the third is both less predictable and more inevitable in retrospect.

## 5. Environment / atmosphere findings

Author directly contrasts generic AI-like environment description with emotionally directed human description: the same wind/environment should be perceived according to the current scene’s emotional meaning.

Distilled rule candidate:
Environment should be focalized. A detail is selected because this character, under this pressure, would notice it and assign it meaning.

Micro-audit:
- whose perception is selecting this detail?
- why now?
- does it alter orientation, threat, clue, choice, memory, mood, or action surface?
- could the same paragraph be pasted into five unrelated scenes? If yes, fail specificity.

Important caution:
“emotional environment” does not mean personifying every object or making every sentence poetic. Page-cost still applies.

## 6. Worldbuilding / rules findings

### 6.1 Iceberg terminology
A visible term can later be recontextualized as only one viewpoint-dependent label for a larger phenomenon.

Transfer value:
- early terminology need not explain the full ontology;
- later reveal should deepen meaning without making earlier use fraudulent.

### 6.2 Rules create moral pressure
The work’s strongest rule design is not just puzzle difficulty. Author frames many rules as almost-possible demands that force people to reveal values under pressure.

FQ001 transfer:
A Mingrida rule/restriction should be audited for:
`mechanical consequence + behavioral consequence + moral/relationship consequence`.
If it only explains interface mechanics, it is underused.

### 6.3 Complexity is a style advantage and a failure risk
Author openly identifies complexity as a personal differentiator. That does not make complexity a universal rule.

Anti-overfit:
FQ001 should seek `depth per rule` before `more rules`. Prefer expanding consequences of known mechanics to adding terminology.

## 7. Plot / long-serial architecture findings

### 7.1 Finite mainline under episodic surface
Author explicitly says the games/character stories are finite because there is a continuing escape/main mystery line.

Transfer principle:
- unit engines must consume or transform finite debts;
- serial fiction should not feel infinitely extendable at unchanged stakes.

### 7.2 Long TOC shows engine rotation
The official TOC alternates game/problem chapters, relationship/team chapters, character-biography chapters, rule/world revelations and larger strategic conflict.

Hypothesis:
retention comes partly from engine rotation, not simply faster pacing.

FQ001 experiment:
front arc and later volume maps should tag dominant engine per chapter/sequence:
`SURVIVAL / MYSTERY / RELATIONSHIP / CHARACTER / WORLD / MORAL_CHOICE / ACTION / AFTERSHOCK`.
Overconcentration triggers pacing-engine review.

## 8. Quality / anti-pattern findings

Do not promote these as universal strengths:
- complexity for its own sake;
- protagonist omniscience justified only retrospectively;
- repeated game/autobiography engines without variation;
- prolonged setup because “the later payoff explains it”;
- reader-comment popularity treated as truth;
- emotional damage used repeatedly without recovery/contrast;
- unresolved or overgrown mystery debt.

Public reader responses include praise for long-range foreshadowing/group characterization and criticism of dragging, protagonist over-centralization and unresolved/unsatisfying late questions. These are not objective verdicts, but they are useful negative-space evidence.
